## 1. Purpose

B1 is an independent empirical check of the principal-track A2 scaling rule. It does not rerun the A2 script or
re-derive A2. Stage 7 established, on the RX product landscape, that typical Loschmidt and SWAP gradient
costs grow like about 4^n and about 16^n. A2 then predicted that what generalises is a relation between
exponents, not those bases. B1 tests that relation on two entangling circuit families, three depth regimes and
three parameter positions, with 10 independent seeds and confidence intervals. It also keeps a mandatory
regression arm on the exact Stage 7 benchmark, gated against the B3 hardened intervals.

B1 makes no novelty claim and no novelty decision, and does not edit Satyabrat's principal conclusions.

## 2. Principal A2 prediction being independently tested

From `principal/QLO_Principal_Track.md` §3 ("Result 1" and "Slope rule"), for a pure-state fidelity objective
F(θ) = |⟨φ|ψ(θ)⟩|², cost C = 1 − F, and the ordinary two-term shift on a Pauli-rotation parameter θ_k:

- F± = F(θ ± π/2 e_k), S = F₊ + F₋, Δ = F₋ − F₊, r = Δ/S, g = Δ/2.
- Exact variances: Var_LE = [F₊(1−F₊)+F₋(1−F₋)]/(4M) and Var_SWAP = [2−F₊²−F₋²]/(4M).
- Exact shot ratio at any SNR ρ: M_SWAP/M_LE = (2−Q)/(S−Q), Q = F₊²+F₋². This is identity C5 in principal §2.
- Concentrated regime (S ≪ 1): M_LE ≈ ρ²/(r²S) and M_SWAP ≈ 2ρ²/(r²S²).
- With median log10 S = a_S − b_S n and median log10|r| = a_r − b_r n:
  - b_LE = b_S + 2b_r,
  - b_SWAP = 2b_S + 2b_r,
  - b_SWAP − b_LE = b_S (**the main B1 equation**).
- The exponent ratio b_SWAP/b_LE equals 2 exactly only when b_r = 0.

The exact identity holds for every θ, with no regime condition. The slope rule is a concentrated-regime statement
about typical (median) behaviour. B1 keeps the two separate.

## 3. Sign-convention correction in the current principal B1 bullet

`principal/QLO_Principal_Track.md` §6, B1 bullet (line 250), reads "The prediction to test is
b_SWAP = b_LE − b_S". Principal §3 derives b_SW = 2b_S + 2b_r = (b_S + 2b_r) + b_S = b_LE + b_S. With every b
defined positive for decay of S and |r|, or for growth of the shot cost, the §3 form is the correct one: SWAP's
exponent is larger than Loschmidt's by the decay rate of S. B1 tests the §3 form, b_SWAP − b_LE = b_S, and the data
confirm the sign (§17).

The principal file is **not** edited. The typo is logged only here, as instructed. B5's line check on the B5 branch
flagged the same sign mismatch.

## 6. Entangling family 1: the repo hardware-efficient ansatz (`hea_ring`)

The implementation is `src/qlo/circuits/hardware_efficient.py`, read, not recreated from memory. Each layer applies
RY(θ[l,q,0]) and then RZ(θ[l,q,1]) on every qubit. It then applies CNOT(0,1), CNOT(1,2), …, CNOT(n−2,n−1) and
CNOT(n−1,0), in that order. Target |0^n⟩, θ iid U[−π, π].

The B1 simulator reproduces PennyLane's `qml.state()` of the repo class at machine precision, and its gradients
match PennyLane autograd on the repo class at every audited n (§9). Because the ring CNOTs act sequentially,
qubit 0 influences every qubit within one layer, so "depth 2" is shallow in layers but not in light-cone size.

## 7. Entangling family 2: RX–RY + CZ brickwork (`rxry_czbrick`)

Neither the repo nor the principal work defines a second or "algebra-controlled" ansatz, so that name is not used.
B1 implements the spec's fallback and names it explicitly. Each layer applies RX(θ[l,q,0]) and then RY(θ[l,q,1])
on every qubit, followed by open-boundary CZ brickwork: even layers CZ(0,1), CZ(2,3), …; odd layers CZ(1,2),
CZ(3,4), ….

It differs from Family 1 in its rotation set, its diagonal entangler, its nearest-neighbour topology without
wrap-around, and its light cone, which grows by about one qubit per layer. It is checked against an independent
PennyLane template (`qlo.b1.audit.pl_rxry_czbrick`).

## 10. Fidelity concentration b_S

S decays exponentially in every entangling cell. The fits have mean R² ≥ 0.9997 and the median S falls
monotonically from about 0.1 at n = 4 to 2e-6–1e-4 at the largest n (`figures/median_S_vs_n.png`). b_S is a
property of the circuit family and depth. It does not depend on the shifted parameter: the three positions agree
to ≤0.001 decades/qubit.

| family | depth 2 | depth 4 | depth n |
|---|---|---|---|
| HEA | 0.376 | 0.319 | 0.303 |
| RX–RY + CZ | 0.382 | 0.340 | 0.305 |
| RX product | — | — | 0.601 (n = 2..20) |

These values are for the primary range. In the secondary range (n ≥ 8), depth n gives 0.302 (HEA) and 0.300
(RX–RY + CZ).

The deep circuits approach log10 2 = 0.301, the value for random states with F ~ 2^−n. Shallow circuits sit in
between, at about 10^0.38 ≈ 2.4 per qubit. None of the entangling families shows the product circuit's log10 4.
Mean scale and log-typical scale are kept separate in `cell_diagnostics_pooled.csv` (mean S, median S, mean and
median log10 S, geometric mean, IQR); every b_S above is a median (log-typical) slope.

## 11. Relative-gradient concentration b_r

**b_r ≈ 0 everywhere.**
- Primary range: |b_r| ≤ 0.0029 decades/qubit and |b_r|/b_S ≤ 0.0096 in all 16 entangling cells.
- Secondary range: |b_r| ≤ 0.0023.
- Typical |r| is flat in n. The median |r| per cell lies between 0.29 and 0.56.
- The median log10|r| barely moves across n: −0.26 to −0.31 (HEA), −0.30 to −0.35 (RX–RY + CZ EARLY and MIDDLE),
  and −0.51 to −0.53 (RX–RY + CZ LATE).
- The fraction of |r| < 1e-3 is at most 0.6% in any cell.

A few b_r CIs exclude 0 by tiny amounts:
- HEA d2 EARLY −0.0007, HEA d2 LATE −0.0004, HEA d4 LATE −0.0005, HEA dn MIDDLE −0.0009;
- RX–RY + CZ d4 LATE +0.0007, RX–RY + CZ dn MIDDLE −0.0029.

The largest of these, RX–RY + CZ dn MIDDLE (−0.0029, primary; −0.0023 for n ≥ 8), is a slight **growth** of |r| with
n. It is 1% of b_S. No cell shows the "relative gradient concentrates" behaviour that would break exponent
doubling.

## 14. Exact ratio identity check

On every non-degenerate θ, M_SWAP/M_LE = (2−Q)/(S−Q) holds in all 6,740,000 per-θ rows (all families, regimes, n,
positions and seeds, including RX). The check uses two arithmetic paths: the Bernoulli and SWAP-ancilla variances
on one side, and S and Q directly on the other.

| family | max relative error |
|---|---|
| entangling | 8.3e-15 |

No row was excluded: there were no S = 0, Δ = 0 or zero-variance rows anywhere. The maximum absolute error, 296,

## 17. Gap rule test (main B1 equation: b_SWAP − b_LE = b_S)

**Primary acceptance per cell: does the bootstrap 95% CI of ε_gap contain 0?**

| range | ε_gap | CI contains 0 | equivalence |
|---|---|---|---|
| primary | −0.0048 to −0.0015 decades/qubit (−0.5% to −1.3% of b_S) | in none of the 16 entangling cells | all within ±0.02 |
| secondary (n ≥ 8) | −0.0039 to +0.0005 | in 8 of 16 cells | all within ±0.02 |

On the secondary range the 8 consistent cells are the four CONSISTENT cells (HEA dn MIDDLE and LATE, RX–RY + CZ dn
EARLY and LATE) and the four GAP-ONLY cells (HEA d4 EARLY and LATE, RX–RY + CZ d4 LATE, RX–RY + CZ dn MIDDLE).

For the RX product, ε_gap is −0.0070 on 2..20 and −0.0006 [−0.0012, −0.0000] on 8..20. The B1 gap
(b_SWAP − b_LE) is 0.317–0.377 against b_S 0.319–0.382 (`figures/gap_test.png`).

**The deviation is real, small and one-signed.** ε_gap < 0 in every primary cell, so the measured gap is slightly
smaller than b_S. Investigating it (Case D says investigate, not hide) gives an exact decomposition. It was
added post hoc and is labelled as such in `posthoc_gap_decomposition.csv`.

    ε_gap = A + D,  A = slope(median log10 R) − b_S,  D = slope of [median log10 M_SWAP − median log10 M_LE − median log10 R]

(reproduced to 2e-16).

**Component A** is the O(S) term in the identity. Because log R is a decreasing function of S alone, median log10 R
= log10 2 − median log10 S + O(S) exactly, and A → 0 as S → 0.

| A | primary | n ≥ 8 |
|---|---|---|
| entangling | −0.0015 to −0.0026 | −0.0001 to −0.0003 |
| RX | −0.0077 | 0.0000 |

So the A2 statement about the median of log R is confirmed, and its finite-S correction is negligible once the
median S is ≲ 1e-2.

**Component D** is the non-additivity of medians: the difference of two medians is not the median of the
difference.
- It is the dominant remainder for n ≥ 8.
- In shallow circuits it is about −0.003 decades/qubit and does not shrink: d2 −0.0027 to −0.0038; RX–RY + CZ d4
  −0.0010 to −0.0023.
- In deep circuits it is about 0: HEA dn −0.0015 to +0.0006; RX–RY + CZ dn −0.0010 to +0.0001.

D is a property of the *statistic* (difference of medians of two correlated, n-dependent distributions), not of the
exact identity. The "slope rule" holds to first order for medians only when the distributions of log10 S and
log10|r| translate with n without changing shape. Shallow circuits change shape more over 4–16 qubits.

Interpretation: the general A2 rule holds to within about 1% of b_S on every circuit, position and depth tested.
Where the strict CI test rejects it inside the concentrated regime, the cause is identified as the
median-of-difference statistic, not a failure of the identity or of b_r ≈ 0.

## 18. Exponent-doubling conditions

Because b_r ≈ 0 everywhere, the predicted ratio (2b_S+2b_r)/(b_S+2b_r) is 1.996–2.020. The doubling test applies
only when the b_r CI contains 0 and |b_r| ≤ 0.1 b_S:

| range | eligible cells | observed b_SWAP/b_LE | consistent with 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| primary | 10 of 16 | 1.969–1.990 | 0 |
| n ≥ 8 | 14 of 16 | 1.977–2.001 | 4 (HEA dn MIDDLE and LATE, RX–RY + CZ dn EARLY and LATE) |

Ineligible cells failed only on the CI condition, because their |b_r| is tiny.

**The exponent is doubled to within 1.5%.** The shortfall of the observed ratio below 2 (−0.01 to −0.03) is the
same ε effect as §§15–17 (b_LE slightly above b_S), not a nonzero b_r. It shrinks with n, and is gone for deep
circuits at n ≥ 8. With the bases: SWAP's per-qubit factor is roughly the square of Loschmidt's in every family
(2.4² ≈ 5.7, 2.1² ≈ 4.4, 2.0² ≈ 4.0). The 4 and 16 of the RX product do not carry over.

Setup: n = 12, seed 11000, every family × regime × distinct position (16 cells). In each cell, the θ rows closest

## 22. Finite-n limitations

- **Short ranges.** The entangling fits use 7 n values (depth 2 and 4) or 6 (depth = n). The secondary range has 5
  or 4 points, so slow curvature cannot be fully separated from a line. The curvature diagnostic and the second
  range are reported for that reason.
- **The small-n points are transitional.** The median S at n = 4 is about 0.1, so the O(S) term (component A, §17)
  bends the primary-range slopes by about 0.002 decades/qubit. It vanishes for n ≥ 8.
- **Medians of differences are not differences of medians.** Component D (§17) persists over n = 8–16 in shallow
  circuits. Whether it decays at larger n is not established.
- **Positions move with n in the depth = n regime**, and depth 2 has only two distinct positions.
- **No statement is made beyond n = 16** (n = 14 for depth = n).

## 23. Numerical limitations

- **Precision.** complex128 statevectors.
  - The F± cross-checks agree to ≤1.1e-13·S in the audit and ≤1.95e-12·S over the whole sweep (bracket vs forward
    F(θ)).
  - The smallest median S is 2.4e-6; nothing is near underflow.
- **Identity tolerance.** R_identity subtracts S − Q, which loses digits when F± is near 0 or 1. The worst case is
  1.75e-11 (RX, n = 2), against a tolerance of 1e-10.
- **Ceil convention.** Used only against B3. For M ≳ 1e13, ceil(M) can move by one integer between arithmetic
  paths, which shifts log10 M by about 1e-14.
- **Bootstrap with 10 seeds.** The percentile bootstrap of a mean over 10 units slightly under-covers, so the
  nominal 95% CIs are probably narrower than true 95%. This makes the primary "CI contains 0" test stricter. The
  predeclared rule was applied as written. The θ bootstrap (§20) confirms that the seed spread is sampling noise.
- **OLS over n.** Slopes are OLS fits of per-seed medians. The CIs come from independent seeds, not from OLS
  standard errors.
- **Kernel and batch changes** (§4) were validated to 4.5e-14·S before use.

## 25. What B1 supports

- The exact shot-ratio identity M_SWAP/M_LE = (2−Q)/(S−Q) holds per θ on every circuit, position and depth tested.
  The worst relative error is 1.75e-11.
- The general A2 slope rule b_SWAP − b_LE ≈ b_S, b_LE ≈ b_S + 2b_r holds to within about 1% of b_S on two
  structurally different entangling families, at depths 2, 4 and n, and at early, middle and late parameters, with
  10-seed CIs.
- The relative gradient does not concentrate (b_r ≈ 0) at any tested position, so the shot exponent of SWAP is
  close to twice that of Loschmidt (ratio 1.97–2.02) in every cell.
- The bases are circuit-dependent. Deep circuits give about 2 per qubit (Loschmidt) and about 4 per qubit (SWAP);
  shallow ones give about 2.4 and 5.7. The RX product's 4 and 16 do not generalise.
- The generalised conditional sign law, (1+|r|)/2 for M·S ≪ 1, holds with error proportional to M·S.
- The B1 harness reproduces Stage 7 and B3 exactly on the RX product.

## 26. What B1 does not establish

- Asymptotic exponents beyond n = 16 (n = 14 for depth = n), or any proof that b_S → log10 2 for deep circuits.
- That ε_gap → 0 for shallow circuits. The median non-additivity term persists over n = 8–16 at depth 2 and 4.
- Behaviour under noise (B4), with other estimators (B2), with shared or adaptive shot allocation, or with
  multi-eigenvalue generators.
- Behaviour for targets other than |0^n⟩ or for non-fidelity costs.
- Other ansatz families. B1 tests two, and the second is a named fallback, not an "algebra-controlled" family.
- Anything about novelty or priority. B1 makes no novelty claim.
