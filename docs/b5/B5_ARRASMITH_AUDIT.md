## 2. Gradient-free cost-difference resolution vs parameter-shift gradient estimation

The paper's analytical result concerns **exact cost differences between two parameter points**. It does not treat
**finite-shot parameter-shift gradient estimators**. The two must not be conflated.

| Aspect | Arrasmith 2021 (Prop. 1, Cor. 1) | Stage 7 (parameter-shift gradients) |
|---|---|---|
| Random quantity | Exact ΔC = C(θ_B) − C(θ_A), random through θ_A (and L, ℓ̂) | Estimator ĝ = (Ĉ₊ − Ĉ₋)/2, random through measurement outcomes at fixed θ |
| Probability space | Parameters θ (landscape statistics) | Shots, at fixed θ; then the distribution over θ |
| Statistic | Mean and variance over θ; Chebyshev bound | Exact pmf, P(ĝ = 0), P_correct, SNR; median required M over θ |
| Points compared | Any two points at distance L ∈ O(poly(n)) | Two shifted points θ ± (π/2)e_k, a specific pair at distance π |
| Measurement model | None in the analysis; numerics sample a local cost with N shots per evaluation | Loschmidt projector (0/1) or SWAP ancilla (±1), M shots per shift |
| Output | Precision needed to resolve ΔC must be exponential: ∝ √G(n) in standard deviation (our reading of Eq. 12); no shot formula | Required M per θ: ∝ 1/(As²) (LE), ∝ 1/(A²s²) (SWAP); medians ∝ 4ⁿ and 16ⁿ |

- **Why they are related.** A parameter-shift component is half a cost difference: g = [C(θ + π/2 e_k) − C(θ − π/2 e_k)]/2.
  Prop. 1 with L = π covers this pair as a special case, at the level of exact values over θ. *(Our observation; the
  paper does not single out shift pairs.)*
- **Why they differ.** Prop. 1 bounds how small exact differences typically are. It says nothing about how a finite-shot
  estimate of the difference is distributed, which depends on the readout.
  - Two readouts of the same ΔC can have different per-shot variances: Stage 7's F(1−F) vs 1 − F².
  - Prop. 1 is identical for both. It cannot produce a readout-dependent exponent.
- **The gradient-descent reference in Fig. 3** ("a custom gradient-descent optimizer", p. 7) is the paper's only
  gradient-based result. Its gradient estimator, shift rule and per-component shot allocation are not specified
  (C-04b). It shows exponential growth of the median N_total, consistent with but not comparable to Stage 7 (§4 below).

## 3. What the key terms mean in this paper

- **"Exponential precision"** (abstract, p. 1; §1, p. 1): the resolution needed to tell cost values apart, scaling like
  the exponentially small standard deviation of ΔC over random points (Prop. 1). It is not tied to a measurement model.
  The base of the exponential is the generic b > 1 of Definition 1.
- **"Shots"**:
  - N is the number of measurement shots allocated **per cost-function evaluation**. For each n, several N were tried
    and the one minimising the median N_total (while reaching C = 0.4) was kept (p. 7).
  - N_total is the total number of shots used throughout one optimisation run.
  - The reported statistic is the **median N_total over 20 runs** (Fig. 3).
  - Shots are not counted per gradient component. The cost observable is built from single-qubit projectors (Eq. 15);
    the measurement circuit is not described (an efficient circuit is cited to ref. [12], p. 7).
- **"Optimisation progress"**: reaching C = 0.4. This threshold was chosen deliberately high, to probe "whether one can
  get past the barren plateau" rather than to find a minimum (p. 7).
- **"Random walk"**: one sentence, §3.2, p. 6 (C-05a). See §5. No random-walk model, control or numerics accompany it.

## 5. The "mentioned in passing" random-walk remark

- **Citing passage.** Aghaei Saem et al. (QST 11, 015049; version of record §1, p. 2; arXiv v2 §I, p. 2) write that
  random-walk behaviour of vanilla gradient descent on barren plateaus "has been mentioned in passing in the literature
  (for example, see [38])". Their [38] is this paper (checked against the version-of-record reference list).
- **The precise sentence.** It is located in §3.2 "Implications for gradient-free optimizers", the opening paragraph,
  last sentence, p. 6 (LaTeX line 564).
  - It states that when an optimizer's precision requirements are not met, each decision is randomly chosen by shot
    noise.
  - It ends "leading to many optimizers effectively becoming either random walks or random sampling".
- **Uniqueness.** It is the only occurrence of "random walk" (and of "random sampling") in the paper and its LaTeX
  source.
- **What it is.**
  - A single qualitative sentence: no proof, model, numerics or control.
  - It sits in the gradient-free section, though phrased for "an optimizer" in general.
  - It concerns decisions randomised by shot noise, not a statistical-indistinguishability statement.
- **Relation to Aghaei Saem's Corollary 2/4 and to Stage 7 §17.**
  - Aghaei Saem's Corollary 2/4 proves indistinguishability from a random walk for parameter-shift gradient descent with
    Pauli observables (B-06a).
  - Stage 7 §17 measures it for SWAP-driven gradient descent with a matched random-walk control.
  - Arrasmith's sentence is the qualitative antecedent the former cites. It contains neither statement.
- Classification: **RELATED BUT DIFFERENT** (matrix row 8).
